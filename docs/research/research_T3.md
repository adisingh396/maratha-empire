# Cluster T3 Research — MYS (Mysore), MDR (Madras/Tamilakam), RJP (Rajputana), WIS (Gujarat)

> **Sourcing:** Validated via chrome-devtools + Wikipedia reads (live search MCPs down). No fabricated names.

## MYS — Mysore (Karnataka; Bangalore, aviation/hydro)
### Historical figures (validated)
- **Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV** (1884–1940) — 24th Maharaja, reformer "Rajarshi", model industrial princely state. HOI4: country_leader. Ideology: authoritarian (enlightened despot).
- **Jayachamarajendra Wadiyar** (1919–1974) — 25th & last Maharaja (from Aug 1940), musician-scholar. HOI4: country_leader (heir) / high_command. Ideology: authoritarian.
- **Sir M. Visvesvaraya** (1861–1962) — 19th Dewan (1912–18), engineer-statesman: KRS dam, Bhadravati iron works, Mysore University. HOI4: political_advisor / theorist (engineering).
- **Sir Mirza Ismail** (1883–1959) — 22nd Diwan (1926–41), later Diwan of Jaipur & Hyderabad; city planner. HOI4: political_advisor / head-of-government token.
- **Sir Albion Rajkumar Banerjee** (1871–1950) — Dewan preceding Mirza Ismail. HOI4: political_advisor.
- **Sir N. Madhava Rao** (1889–?) — Dewan succeeding Mirza Ismail (1941). HOI4: political_advisor.
- **Walchand Hirachand** (1882–1953) — industrialist; founded Hindustan Aircraft (HAL) Bangalore 1940, Scindia Steam Navigation, Premier Automobiles. HOI4: political_advisor (aviation/war industry).
- **Kantirava Narasimharaja Wadiyar** (Yuvaraja, 1888–1940) — heir-apparent lineage. HOI4: high_command / royal token.
- **Krishnaraja Wadiyar III** (1794–1868) — earlier monarch, cultural patron. HOI4: legacy advisor.
- **Tipu Sultan** (1751–1799) — "Tiger of Mysore," rocket-artillery pioneer. HOI4: legacy theorist (rocketry/artillery).
- **Hyder Ali** (1720–1782) — Mysore military modernizer. HOI4: legacy theorist.

### What-if scenarios (grounded)
- **The Engineer-King's Five-Year Plan** — Visvesvaraya's "Reconstruction of India" (1920) + Bhadravati steel + KRS hydro become a state-driven industrialization arc. Authoritarian-technocratic.
- **Hindustan Aircraft at Bangalore** — Walchand's 1940 HAL founding brought forward: Mysore becomes the subcontinent's aviation arsenal.
- **The Regency Question** — Krishnaraja IV's death (1940) & young Jayachamaraja's accession triggers succession/regency event; strengthen monarchy vs empower Diwan.
- **Mysore Model vs Congress** — princely paternalism vs nationalist agitation: co-opt reformers, grant assembly, or repress.
- **Sandalwood & Gold Republic** — Kolar Gold Fields + sandalwood/silk monopoly fund a war chest; resource-extraction spirits.

### Economic & cultural flavor hooks
- KRS dam & Cauvery hydro; Bhadravati (Mysore Iron & Steel); Kolar Gold Fields (world's deepest mines); HAL Bangalore; Mysore silk & sandalwood; Mysore University; Dasara royal pageantry; Cauvery water dispute with Madras (border-tension hook).

## MDR — Madras Presidency / Tamilakam (Chennai; textiles, ports, Dravidian politics)
### Historical figures (validated)
- **Periyar E. V. Ramasamy** (1879–1973) — Self-Respect Movement, Justice Party head (1939), founded Dravidar Kazhagam (1944); anti-caste, Tamil sovereignty ("Dravida Nadu"). HOI4: country_leader. Ideology: populist-authoritarian OR radical-socialist Dravidian.
- **C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji)** (1878–1972) — statesman, conservative-liberal free-marketeer (later Swatantra). HOI4: country_leader. Ideology: democratic/liberal.
- **S. Rm. M. Annamalai Chettiar** (1881–1948) — Raja of Chettinad, Nattukottai banker; founded Indian Bank & Annamalai University. HOI4: political_advisor (finance).
- **P. T. Rajan** (1892–1974) — Justice Party leader, CM of Madras. HOI4: political_advisor / country_leader (Justice Party). Ideology: authoritarian-conservative.
- **Raja of Bobbili (Ramakrishna Ranga Rao)** (1889–1978) — Justice Party Premier of Madras (1932–36). HOI4: country_leader / advisor. Ideology: authoritarian-conservative.
- **C. N. Annadurai** (1909–1969) — Dravidian orator, later founded DMK. HOI4: political_advisor (populist agitator). Ideology: populist-socialist.
- **Sir P. Theagaraya Chetty** (1852–1925) — Justice Party co-founder, industrialist. HOI4: legacy advisor.
- **Dr. C. Natesa Mudaliar** (1875–1937) — Justice Party co-founder. HOI4: political_advisor.
- **V. O. Chidambaram Pillai** (1872–1936) — "Kappalottiya Tamizhan," founded Swadeshi Steam Navigation (Tuticorin). HOI4: navy / political_advisor (merchant marine).
- **Alagappa Chettiar** (1909–1957) — Chettiar industrialist-philanthropist, shipping/insurance. HOI4: political_advisor (industry).
- **M. A. Muthiah Chettiar** (1905–1984) — Raja of Chettinad line, minister-industrialist. HOI4: political_advisor.
- **S. Satyamurti** (1887–1943) — Congress orator, Madras nationalist. HOI4: political_advisor (democratic).

### What-if scenarios (grounded)
- **Dravida Nadu Rising** — Periyar's Dravidistan demand becomes a secession/state-formation arc; anti-caste reform spirits, fork between authoritarian-populist and socialist Dravidian republics.
- **Justice Party Raj** — the non-Brahmin Justice Party governs an independent Tamilakam; communal-reservation focuses, tension with Brahmin/Congress bloc.
- **The Chettiar Banking Empire** — Nattukottai Chettiar capital across Burma/Malaya/Ceylon becomes economic-hegemony branch; overseas-investment spirits, SE-Asia soft power.
- **Rajaji's Free-Market Republic** — Rajagopalachari's economic liberalism anchors a democratic industrial state vs statist planning.
- **The Southern Dockyards** — Madras Harbour + Visakhapatnam (Vizag shipyard 1941) drive a naval-industrial branch.
- **Cauvery Casus Belli** — Cauvery dispute with Mysore becomes a diplomatic-crisis/border event.

### Economic & cultural flavor hooks
- Coimbatore & Madurai cotton mills ("Manchester of South India"); Buckingham & Carnatic Mills; Chettiar banking (Chettinad) & SE-Asia diaspora finance; Madras Harbour + Visakhapatnam shipyard; Tuticorin/Nagapattinam ports; Self-Respect Movement & anti-caste politics; Tamil-vs-Brahmin/Telugu tension; Dravidian rationalist spirit; Kanchipuram silk; leather/tanning (North Arcot).

## RJP — Rajputana (Marwari finance + desert martial; Jaipur/Jodhpur/Bikaner/Udaipur)
### Historical figures (validated)
- **Ganga Singh** (1880–1943) — Maharaja of Bikaner (1888–1943); founded Bikaner Camel Corps (Ganga Risala), built Gang Canal, only Indian in Imperial War Cabinet. HOI4: country_leader / field_marshal. Ideology: authoritarian (martial monarch).
- **Man Singh II (Sawai)** (1912–1970) — Maharaja of Jaipur (1922–48); soldier-king, modernized Jaipur State Forces. HOI4: country_leader / general. Ideology: authoritarian.
- **Umaid Singh** (1903–1947) — Maharaja of Jodhpur (1918–47); aviation enthusiast (Jodhpur Flying Club), Jodhpur railways. HOI4: country_leader / air_chief. Ideology: authoritarian.
- **Bhupal Singh** (1884–1955) — Maharana of Udaipur/Mewar (1930–55); senior-most Rajput ruler (Sisodia). HOI4: country_leader. Ideology: authoritarian/traditionalist.
- **Fateh Singh of Mewar** (1849–1930) — refused Delhi Durbar homage — Rajput-pride legacy token. HOI4: legacy advisor.
- **G. D. Birla** (1894–1983) — Marwari from Pilani; textile/jute/auto/aluminium magnate, Hindustan Motors. HOI4: political_advisor (industry/finance).
- **Jamnalal Bajaj** (1889–1942) — Marwari industrialist (Sikar), Bajaj group, Congress financier. HOI4: political_advisor (finance).
- **Sadul Singh** (1902–1950) — Maharaja Kumar of Bikaner, commanded Ganga Risala. HOI4: general / high_command.
- **Hanwant Singh** (1923–1952) — heir of Jodhpur, aviator. HOI4: high_command / air token.
- **Sir Pratap Singh** (1845–1922) — legendary Jodhpur regent-soldier, raised Jodhpur Lancers. HOI4: legacy field_marshal / theorist (cavalry).
- **Marwari houses — Poddar / Singhania / Goenka** — Shekhawati merchant-banking. HOI4: political_advisor (finance) tokens.
- **Maharaja Sardar Singh of Jodhpur** (1880–1911) — raised Jodhpur Lancers. HOI4: legacy general.

### What-if scenarios (grounded)
- **The Rajput Confederation** — Chamber of Princes dynamic: Jaipur, Jodhpur, Bikaner, Udaipur federate under a war-council; martial-nobility spirit + cavalry/camel-corps doctrine. Mewar's seniority sparks a leadership dispute.
- **Ganga Risala Rides Again** — Bikaner's Camel Corps expands into a desert mechanized-cavalry force; desert-warfare doctrine + Gang Canal agrarian-supply focus.
- **Marwari Capital vs Desert Crown** — industrialist Marwari houses (Birla, Bajaj) financing modernization vs conservative princely courts; empower merchant capital vs feudal control.
- **The Jodhpur Air Arm** — Umaid Singh's aviation passion becomes a princely air force.
- **Canal Wars of the Thar** — Bikaner Gang Canal / Sutlej water: conflict with Punjab over irrigation.
- **The Purdah Reform Crisis** — modernizing rulers vs orthodox nobility over social reform.

### Economic & cultural flavor hooks
- Marwari banking/hundi networks (Shekhawati seths; Birla/Bajaj/Poddar/Singhania); Bikaner Ganga Risala camel corps & Gang Canal; Jodhpur/Jaipur State Forces & Rajput Lancers; salt (Sambhar Lake); marble (Makrana); Thar defensive terrain; Kshatriya warrior-honor spirit; Mewar Sisodia "oldest dynasty" prestige; feudal jagirdari vs modernization tension.

## WIS — Gujarat / Western India (Baroda, Ahmedabad calico, Kathiawar ports)
### Historical figures (validated)
- **Sayajirao Gaekwad III** (1863–1939) — Maharaja of Baroda (1875–1939); compulsory free education, Bank of Baroda (1908), textile industry, patron of Ambedkar. HOI4: country_leader. Ideology: authoritarian-reformist (democratic-leaning).
- **Pratap Singh Rao Gaekwad** (1908–1968) — successor Maharaja (from 1939). HOI4: country_leader (successor). Ideology: authoritarian.
- **Ranjitsinhji Vibhaji II** (1872–1933) — Jam Sahib of Nawanagar (1907–33); legendary cricketer, League of Nations delegate. HOI4: legacy/heritage token (d.1933).
- **Digvijaysinhji Ranjitsinhji Jadeja** (1895–1966) — Jam Sahib of Nawanagar (from 1933); built Bedi port, sheltered Polish refugees. HOI4: country_leader. Ideology: authoritarian/humanitarian.
- **Kasturbhai Lalbhai** (1894–1980) — Ahmedabad textile magnate (Arvind Mills, Atul chemicals). HOI4: political_advisor (textile/industry).
- **Ambalal Sarabhai** (1890–1967) — Ahmedabad mill owner (Calico Mills / Sarabhai group). HOI4: political_advisor (industry).
- **Aga Khan III (Sir Sultan Muhammad Shah)** (1877–1957) — western-India commercial/political figure, League of Nations president. HOI4: political_advisor (diplomacy/finance).
- **Khengarji III** (1866–1942) — Maharao of Kutch (1875–1942); developed Kandla/Mandvi trade. HOI4: country_leader. Ideology: authoritarian.
- **Krishnakumarsinhji** (1912–1965) — Maharaja of Bhavnagar; built Bhavnagar port, first to accede/reform. HOI4: country_leader / advisor.
- **Sir T. Madhav Rao** (1828–1891) — legendary Baroda administrator. HOI4: legacy political_advisor.
- **Pilaji Gaekwad** (d.1732) — Maratha founder of Baroda. HOI4: legacy advisor (Maratha-link).
- **Sir Chinubhai Madhavlal** (1864–1916) — first Ahmedabad mill-owner baronet. HOI4: legacy political_advisor.

### What-if scenarios (grounded)
- **The Gaekwad Enlightenment** — Sayajirao's reforms (free education, Bank of Baroda, women's rights, textiles) drive a modernizing-monarchy arc; education/research + banking spirits. Baroda's Maratha origin links to the Akhand Bharat core.
- **Manchester of the East** — Ahmedabad's calico/textile cluster (Kasturbhai, Sarabhai) becomes an industrial-powerhouse branch; textile-to-war-industry conversion.
- **Kathiawar Maritime League** — Jamnagar (Bedi port), Bhavnagar, Porbandar, Kutch (Kandla/Mandvi) federate into a merchant-marine power; naval-trade & Arabian-Sea commerce.
- **The Diamond Road** — Surat/Kathiawar diamond-cutting & Gulf dhow trade becomes a commercial-empire branch.
- **Baroda Succession & the Maratha Question** — as a Maratha-founded state amid Gujarati kingdoms, Baroda chooses: join the Maratha core or lead an independent Gujarat.
- **Salt & Sea Reform Pressure** — Gujarat salt/coastal economy + nationalist agitation (Gandhi's home region): civil-reform vs princely-authority fork.

### Economic & cultural flavor hooks
- Ahmedabad calico/cotton mills (Arvind, Calico/Sarabhai; Millowners' Association); Bank of Baroda; Sayajirao's free education & libraries; Kathiawar ports (Jamnagar/Bedi, Bhavnagar, Porbandar, Veraval); Kutch (Kandla & Mandvi), Kutchi dhow trade to Africa/Gulf; Surat diamond-cutting; Jain/Bania merchant-prince culture; salt pans; Baroda's Maratha (Gaekwad) heritage as bridge to the Maratha core; Vaishnav/Jain pacifist-commerce vs martial tension.
